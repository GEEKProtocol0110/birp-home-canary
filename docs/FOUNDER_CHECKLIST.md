# BIRP — minimal founder checklist

The immediate path emphasizes work that can be done without buying hardware. This is a proposed practical sequence based on the founder's original minimal-plan request.

- [x] Recover the product concept and record its source.
- [x] Publish a project site and interactive concept demonstration.
- [x] Organize the repository, roadmap, and builder handoff.
- [x] Draft a one-page product overview.
- [x] Recreate and visually review the invention-brief PDF (version 1.0).
- [ ] Choose one initial use case and define the specific problem.
- [ ] Ask prospective users to explain what the bird means; record actual feedback.
- [ ] Consolidate device, system, and app-flow concept diagrams, clearly labeled as concepts.
- [ ] Finish an invention-description packet with dated design decisions and unresolved questions.
- [ ] Prepare a concise partner/licensing pitch and combine the useful materials into one reviewable package.
- [ ] Choose a partner-supported hardware experiment or another resource-appropriate next step.

## Three business questions

1. **Need:** What specific problem makes someone want BIRP?
2. **Discovery:** How will that audience find it?
3. **Economics:** Could a validated design support its full costs?

There are no customer results, manufacturing quotes, signed partners, revenue forecasts, patent filings, or granted rights documented here. Earlier assistant examples are not evidence of any of these. This checklist does not authorize outreach or spending.
